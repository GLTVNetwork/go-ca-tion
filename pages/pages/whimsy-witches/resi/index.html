Whimsy Witches • Schattenwald

Resi

Where the Forest Speaks Softly

Resi has never believed that the forest keeps secrets.

She thinks it simply waits for people to become quiet enough to notice.

Most mornings, before the village has fully awakened, she is already walking the narrow paths that wind between the trees.

Not searching for anything in particular.

Just paying attention.

The first fern uncurling after winter.

Fresh rain caught on a spider's web.

A patch of wild thyme that wasn't there the week before.

To Resi, these are the forest's way of saying good morning.

The Herb Garden

Just beyond the edge of the village sits a small garden surrounded by a low wooden fence.

No sign marks the entrance.

It doesn't need one.

The fragrance usually finds visitors before the path does.

Lavender sways beside sage.

Chamomile nods in the breeze.

Calendula adds splashes of gold among the greens.

Every plant has a purpose.

Some are useful.

Some are simply beautiful.

Resi believes both reasons are equally important.

Forest Remedies

Visitors sometimes imagine they are coming for medicines.

Resi gently corrects them.

"The forest isn't trying to fix everything," she says.

"Sometimes it simply helps us remember how to feel better."

A warm cup of mint tea after a long walk.

Lavender drying near an open window.

Honey stirred into chamomile before bed.

These are not miracles.

They are small kindnesses.

Resi has always trusted small kindnesses.

Learning From the Seasons

No two years are exactly alike in Schattenwald.

Some springs arrive early.

Some linger in winter a little longer.

Resi keeps no calendar.

She watches the blossoms instead.

She notices which birds return first.

She knows when the mushrooms begin appearing beneath the pines.

She can tell from the smell of the earth whether rain will arrive before evening.

The forest has become her oldest teacher.

She is still learning.

Resi and the Whimsy Witches

Among the five friends known as the Whimsy Witches, Resi reminds everyone that the smallest things are often the easiest to overlook.

A single flower.

A quiet path.

A cup of tea shared with a friend.

She has never measured life by grand adventures.

Only by moments worth remembering.
